# Writing a post for samuellbrown.dev

The site is Astro, dark theme only, posts in `src/content/blog/*.md`. The house style was set by
"The collie is the algorithm", "Making a robot collie" and "Picking the puppy" (Aug and Sep 2026).
Read the most recent of those before writing, and match it.

## Voice

Plain, first person, British English. Sam is explaining something he built to a friend who is
technical but wasn't there. Honest about what broke, what didn't matter and what he wanted to be
true but wasn't.

Rules that came from direct feedback, so treat them as hard:

- **No em dashes anywhere.** Not in prose, chart titles, widget labels or tooltips. Use a comma,
  parentheses, "so", "which", or a full stop.
- **No runs of short full-stopped sentences.** "Behaviour didn't beat the score. I wanted it to."
  reads as AI. Join the thought up: "I wanted the behaviour probes to beat the score and they
  didn't, at least not here." Sentences are conversational and often long, glued with commas,
  "and", "so", "because", "which". Asides go in parentheses.
- **No aphorisms or slogans**, especially as the opening of a bullet. "Triage only pays when the
  lottery has blanks in it" and "The optimizer you can forecast is the one that fails most" both
  got rejected. Say what happened instead: "Whether it's worth rehoming runs early depends on how
  many of them were going to fail anyway."
- **No punchy fragments** ("Three shapes.", "It's a staircase, not a slope.") and no hooks of the
  "the question the big labs pay millions over" kind.
- **No AI connectives** like "The game is anecdote, so here's the measurement" or "that's been
  nagging at me". If a sentence exists only to bridge two sections, delete it.
- **Bold only for named things** (a rule, a sheep type, an optimizer in a list, a button label),
  never for emphasis in prose.
- **No killing.** Runs and dogs are never killed or culled. Use "rehome", "give up on it", "keep
  or rehome". This came from Sam and applies to code comments and generated chart text too.
- **Text only where it really needs it.** Sam cut a five-sentence paragraph to two, and called four
  consecutive paragraphs of findings "too much text all in one go, very hard to read". A paragraph
  that lists numbers should be a table, tile row, chart or card grid instead. The prose that survives
  is the reasoning a graphic can't carry: why something didn't work, what the best guess is, what
  changed your mind. Reflections are short.
- **Numbers stay exact and traceable.** Every figure in the prose must come from the experiment
  script's output, seeded, and match the tables and charts.

Sam's earlier posts show the voice well. From the collie post: "Most of the work is done by the
sheep, not the dog. If each sheep pays attention to fewer than about half the flock, no dog can pen
them, however clever it is. Nearly every time I thought the dog had a bug, the actual problem was
in the sheep."

## Structure

Demo first, numbers second, reflection last.

1. **Intro** opens with the question the post answers, in one or two sentences, then a line on how
   it was tried. Front matter: `title`, `publishDate`, a `description` block written in the same
   voice, `tags`.
2. **Setup** as a number-tile flow (`.fc-flow` of `.fc-step` cards, a big number and a short label
   each) rather than a paragraph. If there are named methods or variants, they get bold cards
   (`.fc-methods`) so they stand out.
3. **Three or four interactive demos**, as a step-by-step recipe: the basic thing, the best version
   found, where it fails, the fix, then a look inside ("What it's thinking"). Each demo is a raw HTML
   mount point with `data-*` roles, a HUD line, and controls in `.sheepdog-controls`.
4. **The numbers.** Charts and tables generated by the experiment script, a limitations list,
   and a "what this looks like at scale" comparison if there is one.
5. **What I took from it.** Short. Prefer a graphic (see below) over bullets. If bullets, each is one
   joined-up thought with its number in it, no slogans.
6. **Sources line** in italics at the very end: the previous posts, the experiment script and the
   demo code on GitHub, then the papers.

## Where to use a graphic instead of words

Sam's default is "show it, don't list it". Concretely:

- **Setup facts** (how many runs, generations, flocks, seconds): number tiles, not prose.
- **Named methods or variants**: a row of bold cards with one sentence each.
- **Any paragraph with three or more numbers**: a table or a chart.
- **Comparisons across a range** (by generation, by size): a line chart with a crosshair tooltip.
- **When something happened** (the jump, the failure): a stacked histogram, not a spaghetti chart.
  A hundred overlaid lines was called "hard to read".
- **Big tables**: put them in a `<details class="demo-box">` popup with a title and one-line
  description in the summary, not inline. The chart stays inline, the table backs it up.
- **The conclusion**: a grid of cards (`.fc-takeaways` of `.fc-take`), each with a headline number,
  one or two plain sentences, and a tiny horizontal bar chart of the two to four numbers behind it.
- **A widget that only prints a sentence** from selectors "doesn't make sense for showing the
  data". Show the curve or the bars, and put the sentence in a caption.
- **Two shapes of thing** (smooth vs jagged, easy vs hard): side-by-side `.fc-card`s with a small
  sketch svg at the top.
- **One finding per measured thing** (a signal, a probe, a variant): a grid of small cards
  (`.fc-signals` of `.fc-signal`), each with a colour swatch matching the main chart, a sparkline of
  that thing's curve, its value at the generation the post is about, and one plain sentence. This
  replaced four paragraphs in the puppy post. Build the sparklines from the same JSON the chart uses,
  and put one transparent `data-tip` rect over each so hovering gives the numbers.
- **Rule of thumb:** if a paragraph names more than two numbers, or walks through several things one
  after another, it wants to be a graphic with a sentence under it, not a paragraph.

Every chart gets a tooltip. The experiment script's `chart()` embeds its series in `data-lines` and
`bars()` puts `data-tip` on each segment; `mountFigures()` in the post's sim JS renders the
tooltip for any `[data-tip]` element inside `figure.robot-figure svg`. New graphics should reuse
that rather than add another mechanism. State badges on demo panels are absolute-positioned
overlays, never in-flow text, so nothing shifts when they appear.

Colours: purple `#a93fe0`, orange `#c97c12`, green `#35a066` for series, bright accent
`--accent-dark` (`#c561f6`) for the highlighted thing, `#4c5470` for the greyed comparison,
labels `#8490b5`, values `#e9e6dd`. Theme trap: `--accent-light` is the dark purple on this site,
so anything that must stand out uses `--accent-dark`.

## Demos

- Vanilla JS in `public/sim/`, no bundler. The core model is a pure ES module that also runs
  headless in Node, so the experiment script in `scripts/` uses the same code as the page.
  Seeded PRNG throughout so every number is reproducible.
- Interactive things run to a user-chosen amount and stop. Nothing runs forever.
- The learned or simulated thing keeps realistic manners built into the world (max speed, stand-off
  from sheep, turn limit). Barging or teleporting is a bug, not a feature.
- Show the model's thoughts where there is a model: inputs, hidden units, the chosen action, and a
  reading in the paper's own terms.
- Evolution vocabulary: "evolve", "robot collie", "litter", "puppy", "rehome". Not "breed" and not
  "kill".
- Heavy runs go lighter on phones (smaller batch, progress within a generation).
- CSS for a post lives in a `<style>` block at the bottom of the markdown, scripts in `<script
  src="/sim/..." data-astro-rerun>` tags just above it. `readingTime` already strips those.

## Workflow

- Run `npx astro dev --host 127.0.0.1 --port 4321` in the background and check the post at
  `http://127.0.0.1:4321/blog/<slug>/`. Playwright is in the npx cache
  (`~/.npm/_npx/e41f203b7505f1fb/node_modules/playwright`) for screenshots at 1200 and 390 wide and
  for hovering a bar to confirm the tooltip fires. Look at the screenshots.
- Regenerate charts and tables from the experiment script, never hand-edit numbers.
- Scratch data (run JSON, chart HTML) lives in `scratch/<post>/`, untracked.
- New charts go in the experiment script (`chart()` or `bars()` in `scripts/*-experiments.mjs`),
  regenerated with the script's `analyse` command into `scratch/<post>/chart-*.html`, then pasted
  into the post inside `<figure class="robot-figure" data-chart="name">` with a legend and caption.
- The dev server sometimes keeps serving a stale render after a large edit to a post. Restart it with
  `fuser -k 4321/tcp` and start again rather than debugging the markdown.
- The site is on Astro 7 (Sept 2026): the blog collection uses the glob loader, pages use `entry.id`
  and `render(entry)`, rehype plugins go inside `unified()` in `astro.config.mjs`, and the search on
  the blog index is Pagefind's component UI themed through `--pf-*` variables in
  `src/pages/blog/index.astro`. Any new site chrome must use the site's tokens (`--gray-*`,
  `--accent-dark`, `--font-body`), Sam notices when something "doesn't fit with the colours".
- Do not commit unless Sam asks. He has been asking for pushes at the end of each round of edits;
  push when asked, and push promptly if a change is fixing something visibly broken on the live site.
- After a rewrite, grep the post for `—`, `kill`, `cull`, and for bullets made of short full-stopped
  sentences, before reporting done.
